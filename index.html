A little test

How well do you know Hinduism?

Six questions from the book. Pick an answer, then move on.

1 / 6  ·  According to the Bhagavad Gita, what is true of the soul?

The Gita's central teaching: "The soul is never born nor dies at any time… It is unborn, eternal, ever-existing, and primeval." This insight carried Dr. Shetty through two battles with cancer.

2 / 6  ·  Hinduism has one founder, one holy book, and one central authority — true or false?

Hinduism is what the book calls "open source" — no single founder, no single scripture, no central office. Inclusive, pluralistic, and progressive by construction, with room for the devotee, the philosopher, the yogi, and even the atheist.

3 / 6  ·  How many main paths to liberation (Moksha) does Hinduism recognise?

Four paths: Bhakti (devotion), Karma (action without attachment), Jnana (knowledge), and Raja Yoga (meditation). The book's chapter "Which Path Is Right For You?" helps readers find their own.

4 / 6  ·  At your core, Hinduism says you are —

One of the book's most striking chapters: "You Are Not a Sinner — You Are Divine." There is divinity in everything and everybody. Worship is an invitation, not a summons.

5 / 6  ·  What does "Dharma" most closely mean in the context of this book?

Swami Bodhananda Saraswati describes Dharma in the foreword as "a sustainable way of community living." It is practiced in an ordinary day — in the office, on the commute, in how you treat the person in front of you.

6 / 6  ·  Bhagavad Gita 2.40: "Even a little practice of this dharma —"

"In this path there is no loss of effort, no harm comes of it. Even a little practice of this dharma saves one from great fear." Take a single step, and the divine takes a hundred to meet you where you are.

From the Preface

The book no one had written yet

I grew up in Udupi, a small temple town in southern India — the city of the Krishna Temple and the legacy of Adi Shankaracharya. Hinduism was simply the air around me. I never had to question my identity.

Then I built my life in America, surrounded by people of every other religion — and found that very few of them knew anything about Hinduism. Fewer still of our own children did.

So I wrote the book I had been looking for: a short, honest summary of the essential ideas, in plain language, separated from the rituals that are not essential. A parent can hand it to a child. A child can finish it in an afternoon and feel proud of where they come from.

Alive because of doctors

At peace because of a teaching

Cancer arrived at 41. It came back years later. Twice, what carried me was not a hospital corridor but a single idea I had grown up hearing and had never truly needed until then: that the soul is never born and never dies.

An open religion

Not a franchise — an open source

Hinduism has no single founder, no single book, no central office issuing rulings. It is inclusive, pluralistic and progressive by construction — with room for the devotee, the philosopher, the yogi, and even the atheist.

The paradigm shift

You are not a sinner. You are divine.

There is divinity in everything and everybody. You are not a sinner but a spark of the divine — and worship is an invitation, not a summons. For many readers this one chapter changes the whole frame.

Who this book is for

The young Hindu in America

You grew up going to temple but never learned why. A friend asks, "why do you have so many gods?" — and you have no answer. This book gives you one.

The spouse marrying in

You want to understand your partner's family, not just sit politely through the ceremony. Start here, from the outside in, with nothing assumed.

The seeker without a tradition

Spiritual but not religious. You meditate, you believe in something larger, you belong to nothing. Hinduism has room for you — including if you don't believe in God at all.

The parent, the teacher, the curious

Anyone who needs a short, reliable, non-preaching description of Hinduism to give away — at the kitchen table, in a classroom, or in a temple library.

Practiced at the kitchen table, not only in the temple

You do not need Sanskrit. You do not need a priest, a pilgrimage, or a free weekend. Dharma is something you do in an ordinary day — in the office, on the commute, in how you treat the person in front of you.

Krishna's promise in the Gita is that the approach itself is enough: in whatever way people approach Me, I meet them in that same way. Take a single step, and the divine takes a hundred to meet you where you are.

Swami Bodhananda Saraswati

Hinduism Explained — A Beginner's Guide by late Anup Kumar Shetty MD is an easy-to-read primer of Hindu Dharma, one of the oldest living traditions of the world. Dharma is a complex concept that involves varied beliefs, values, and practices of a mass of people held together by a thread of commonality of purpose and motivation reflected in the stories they tell and dreams they share with each other. This book explores some of those complexities and unpacks the inner meaning of Dharma, which is generally understood as a sustainable way of community living.

About the author

Dr. Anupkumar Shetty, MD (1963–2026)

A physician, a two-time cancer survivor, and a lifelong student of Hinduism. He grew up in Udupi, India — the town of the Krishna Temple and of Adi Shankaracharya — and built his career in medicine in the United States, where he raised his family.

His first book, Hinduism Simplified, was released in India by Sri Sri Sri Vishwesha Teertha Swamiji of Udupi Pejawara Matt and in the USA by Swami Medhananda. He also wrote The Power of Infinity. He remains, in his own words, a student — offering no new information, only a simpler summary of what he believes every person in the world should know about Hinduism.

With a foreword by Swami Bodhananda Saraswati and poems contributed by Dr. Raju.
